Three scans, untouched

Tap any photo to lift the overlay. The heat is face-relative: it marks where this face is denser than its own baseline, which is why a clear face doesn't light up red and a covered one doesn't wash out.

Under it

Three things happen between the shutter and the map

01 · Frame

Find the face, then the skin

478 landmarks set a face-intrinsic coordinate frame, and a face-parsing network cuts hair, brows, lips and eyes out of the mask. Every threshold downstream is measured in millimetres of face, not pixels — so standing closer doesn't change the answer.

02 · Separate

Pull pigment apart from shadow

In optical-density space the melanin and haemoglobin axes are recovered and the shading direction is projected out analytically. A shadow across the cheek stops reading as pigment, and a third of a stop of exposure stops reading as redness.

03 · Read

Predict density, not dots

A face-pretrained encoder with a Poisson density head predicts lesions per area across the face. Asking for a count per region rather than a box per spot is the question the product actually poses — and the one the model can answer.

Measured, not claimed

What it gets right, on faces it has never seen

Held-out faces from a dermatologist-annotated set, scored against the hand-tuned detector that came before it — on the same five zones this app puts on screen.

Method Densest zone named first Densest zone in top two
Learned density model 51% 79%
Hand-tuned detector 25% 47%
Chance 20% 40%

154 faces resolved all five zones. Asked the stricter way — nine anatomical regions instead of five zones — the model still leads, 36% to 20% on the single worst region across 222 faces, with rank correlation +0.56 against +0.20.

It is not a diagnosis. It ranks areas by lesion density. It does not tell acne from post-inflammatory marks from anything that needs a doctor.
→
Beards and stubble read hot. Dark hair inside the skin mask is the largest single source of false heat, and it is visible in one of the scans above.
→
The training set is one population. Held-out accuracy is measured on the same clinical set the model learned from. Consented captures across skin tones are the gate before this goes anywhere.
→
Session to session still drifts. Within one burst the numbers repeat tightly; across days, under different light, they move about five times more. Compare scans taken the same way.
